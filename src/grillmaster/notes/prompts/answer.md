## Research and writing

- Check every fact on the web before you write it (dates, numbers, outcomes, who did what) and give its source in `evidence`: a URL, or the block, card time or frame that shows it. Leave out what you could not confirm.
- Current events are as of the broadcast date: search news from around it, and write what was known then (a later outcome only when the note needs it, dated). Without a date, place the episode in time from its content before you search.
- The earlier passes' reports, when given, cite what they already found on the web (mostly names and terms). Reuse it and research the rest.
- `block` is the block the note shows with: where the viewer first needs it, usually its first mention. When explaining it there would give away a reveal the show builds to, use the block of the reveal.
- `term` is what the note explains, copied exactly as it is written in that block's `ja` or `zh`.
- `text` is the note in Traditional Chinese (Taiwan): the term as the subtitles render it, a full-width colon, then the facts. At most 60 characters, one paragraph, no line break. Render names as the subtitles and the briefing do. Plain and factual: no jokes, no opinions.
- One note per block. Notes on blocks close together crowd the screen: merge them, or keep the one that matters.

Return the notes in `notes`; an empty list is a valid answer.
