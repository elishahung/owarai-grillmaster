Write translator's notes (譯者註) for the Traditional Chinese (Taiwan) subtitles of this Japanese variety show, as a professional subtitle translator would: the shared knowledge a Japanese audience brings to the episode and a Taiwanese audience lacks, given only where the episode leans on it.

The viewer reads Chinese, not Japanese, and knows Japanese variety shows only casually. They read the dialogue subtitles at the bottom of the screen and the translated on-screen cards beside each card. A note shows at the top of the picture for a few seconds from the start of its block and tells, in a sentence or two, what the viewer is missing.

The dialogue table pairs each block's Japanese (ASR) with its final Chinese subtitle. The translated cards are what the viewer reads on screen besides the subtitles. The on-screen text table, when there is one, lists every card the video shows in Japanese, translated or not: evidence, not something the viewer can read.

## When a note is needed

Note something only when all of these hold:

- the talk, a joke or its punchline leans on something a Japanese viewer brings and a Taiwanese viewer lacks:
  - lasting knowledge: a person, a work, an older show, a historical event, a custom, an institution, a place, a product or a phrase;
  - context tied to when the episode aired: what was in the news (society, politics, sports, entertainment), what was trending, and where the people in it stood at the time (their recent work, news or scandals, their standing with each other). Read the talk against the broadcast date, not against today;
- the viewer cannot get it from what they read: the Chinese subtitles around it (read the blocks before and after, to the end of the topic) and the translated cards. A label is not an explanation: a subtitle that names a case or calls someone a suspect still leaves the viewer guessing what happened, so note the fact the moment needs;
- without it the viewer would miss why the moment is funny, shocking or meaningful, not just a detail.

Do not note:

- what the conversation itself explains, earlier or soon after;
- what a translated card already says; when a card covers part of it, note only the part it leaves out;
- a person whose identity does not matter to the moment: a name in passing;
- who a cast member is (anyone appearing in the episode: hosts, guests, performers), however much their style or career bears on the moment: the viewer watches them through the episode. Note one only for where they stood at the time, when the talk leans on it;
- the joke itself: give the background and let the viewer get the joke; never explain why something is funny;
- wordplay the subtitles could not carry, unless a fact behind it is what is missing;
- what a Taiwanese viewer of Japanese variety shows already knows.

When in doubt, leave it out: a few notes that land beat many. An episode may need none.
